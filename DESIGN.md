I set out making my final project as a test to see how far I've come since the beginning of my CS50 journey. I started this class out like everyone who takes it, making something in the programming language Scratch. I got carried away in my first week and a little ambitiously created my own little game where you fly a helicopter around collecting randomly generated orbs and dodging meteors. I wanted to recreate that with some upgrades for my project. I think I succeeded, but there are some upgrades I had to leave on the chopping block given time constraints. Overall, I'm very happy with my final product and I hope you will be as well! I will go through some of my design decisions here to give you a better understanding of my thought process when designing my game.

To start, I was going to switch the genre of this game entirely - at the bidding of my girlfriend - to a horse or a unicorn flying through the sky. I'm actually glad I decided against that and stuck to a similar style as my scratched game as it makes it much more of a stark comparison between the two. I started with polygons on the blank canvas, working on the physics of my game first. A bug that occurred where my jumping animation happened in the sky instead of when on the ground led to me deciding to stick with the helicopter theme and allow for flight all the time. 

I didn't think that adding sprites would be the hardest part of the process, but it turned out to be. Not because adding the sprites was hard, but I learned that making the hit boxes of the sprites line up with where they are in the backend was much harder than I thought it would be. The buildings were some how the hardest part of all of them, which in hindsight, feels stupid because they're just floating rectangles in space that don't move. But the variability of where they spawn in height caused me a lot of problems. The sprites wanted to stretch their scale and fit the whole thing in when I didn't want that. I eventually got it to look how I wanted. Speaking of the buildings, I do think that it's a little strange to have buildings coming from the ceiling, but there are two reasons for this design decision, a technical one and an explanation for it. The first reason is that it simply felt like the game needed that added challenge. If you didn't have the inverted buildings, it was too easy to get through the level. The explanation of why it's happening is that this particular armaggeddon that's happening is an "Inception" style event where the ground is curving up on itself.

I also had some issues with sprite sheets. I found a library online called "anim8" that took sprite sheets and allowed you to flash a portion of a frame every .2 or so seconds (you can change the frame speed) so it looks like it's animated. This was hard as well, because some of the sprite sheets I got weren't lined up correctly, and that caused animation glitches. I had to cutout the sprites and line them up in photoshop in order to create correct sprite sheets. This challenge taught me a lot about how animations work and limitations with anmiations/sprites in relation to hitboxes on the backend. One of the small bugs I left in the game is that the helicopter hit box doesn't line up perfectly with the helicopter, especially when it is rotating to give the sense of helicopter flight. It remains a solid box on the backend, so there are moments you might crash and think that you didn't hit something, unfortunately. I ran out of time to solve that particular hurdle, but I don't think it's a game breaking thing for the hit box to be slightly off.

I thought about creating an options menu to adjust the volume and resolution settings, but it ended up proving to be too much of a challenge and I ran out of time to tackle the issue. Hopefully I made the music and sounds a managable level that isn't too loud, but you're still able to hear it! Also, the resolution issue ended up being too much of a problem as I locked all of the randomly generated items such as the buildings and the meteors to the camera's view and the scale was very off when I tried to play the game at 1920x1080. So instead of changing most of the code, I had to release the game with 800x600 with no option to change. As basically a web game, I felt like that wasn't too much of an issue.

I hope you enjoyed my walkthrough of the challenges I faced with the designing and implementation of my code! It has been a wild ride through CS50, but it was a challenge I am glad I did and hope to continue in my coding journey in the future. Thank you all for being so great in our journey together, you've all helped me get to where I am today and be as comfortable with coding as I am. I definitely had a few weeks in the middle where I thought I was never going to get as comfortable as I am today. Not to say that there isn't more (MUCH MORE) room for growth! Thank you and I hope you enjoyed my game!
